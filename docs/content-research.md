# Content research + proposed prompt bank

Research behind the rewrite of SPILL's truth, dare and 18+ decks.
**Nothing in the app has been changed yet — this is for approval.**

---

## 1. What the evidence actually says

### The single most important finding: Gen Z has a "question deficit"

Hinge's 2025 Gen Z D.A.T.E. report found a measurable gap between the conversations Gen Z
wants and the questions they're willing to ask:

| Finding | Figure |
| --- | --- |
| Believe *they* ask enough questions | 62% (het) / 61% (LGBTQIA+) |
| Say *their dates* ask enough questions | 30% (het) / 25% (LGBTQIA+) |
| More likely to want a second date when asked thoughtful questions | 85% |
| Gen Z men who avoid emotional depth early for fear of seeming "too much" | 48% |
| Gen Z men who say they *do* want those conversations | 65% |

Everyone wants the deeper conversation. Almost nobody wants to be the one who starts it.

**This is the entire reason this product should exist.** SPILL's job is to be the one who
asked. The wheel takes the social risk that no individual in the room wants to take. Every
prompt should be written as something a person would *want* to be asked but would never
ask first.

### The vulnerability asymmetry

- **52%** feel ashamed or second-guess themselves after being vulnerable.
- Only **19%** feel uncomfortable when someone *else* opens up.

People are far harder on themselves than on each other. The fear is real but unfounded, and
a game is the ideal place to prove it — which is an argument for prompts that reward
disclosure with laughter rather than silence.

Hinge's own expert advice was literally *"practice small disclosures in trusted spaces."*
That is a description of this game.

### The escalation ladder is research-backed

Arthur Aron's 1997 study (the "Fast Friends Procedure") produced the famous 36 questions.
The mechanism is **"sustained, escalating, reciprocal, personalistic self-disclosure"** —
three sets of increasing intimacy over 90 minutes.

This validates SPILL's existing intensity system, and tells us how to structure it:

| Aron set | SPILL rounds | Character |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set I | 1–2 | Playful, hypothetical, low cost. *"What would a perfect day look like?"* |
| Set II | 3–5 | Personal history, values, regrets. *"What's your most treasured memory?"* |
| Set III | 6+ | Direct, interpersonal, said to someone's face. *"Tell them what you like about them — be honest this time."* |

The critical detail is that Set III turns **outward**. The deepest questions aren't the most
private ones — they're the ones aimed at the people in the room. Our late-round prompts
should do the same.

### What kinds of questions Gen Z values most

1. **Follow-up questions** to something already said — 61%
2. Questions about **personal interests** — 50%
3. Questions about **personal values** — 49%

Follow-ups winning outright is notable: the prize isn't the question, it's being *dug into*.
Prompts that hand the group a thread to pull will outperform self-contained ones.

### Authenticity has replaced performance

Tinder's Year in Swipe 2025:

- **73%** feel a stronger connection when they can be fully themselves rather than performing.
- **64%** say emotional honesty is what dating needs most; **60%** want clearer intentions.
- **"Friendfluence"** — **42%** say friends influence their dating life, **37%** plan group or
  double dates, **34%** say their friends' relationships give them hope.

Friendfluence is the useful one for us: this is a game played *with the friends whose opinions
already shape these decisions.* Prompts that make the group weigh in are playing to the
strongest card we have.

### Gen Z has precise vocabulary for modern dating pain

The concepts are far more useful than the slang: situationship, the ick, breadcrumbing,
delulu, orbiting, benching, cushioning, zombie-ing, slow fade, soft launch, sneaky link,
freak matching, beige flag, cuffing season, DTR, talking phase, crashout, aura farming.

**Use the concepts, not the words.** The original brief was right that forced slang reads as
cringe — and slang dates fastest. "What's the longest you've been in something you couldn't
name?" beats "tell us about your situationship" and will still make sense in three years.

### Two constraints the data imposes

- **56%** say fear of rejection has stopped them pursuing someone, and **44%** have little to
  no dating experience. A deck that assumes everyone has a rich romantic history will exclude
  nearly half the room. Every crush/dating prompt needs a version answerable by someone who
  has never dated.
- Gen Z is **30%** more likely than millennials to believe in a soulmate and **39%** more
  likely to call themselves romantic idealists. Sincerity lands. Pure cynicism doesn't.

### How adult decks are structured

Adult game publishers tier explicitness rather than mixing it — commonly Romantic → Flirty →
Spicy → Red Hot → Kinky. Truths carry the explicit content; dares carry physical action. The
consent guidance is consistently "read the room, agree banned topics up front."

## 2. Design rules this produces

1. **Ask what they wish someone would ask.** Every prompt should feel like relief, not
   interrogation.
2. **Be specific, and anchor in time.** *"When did you last…"* and *"What's the most recent…"*
   produce stories. *"Do you ever…"* produces "yeah, sometimes."
3. **Leave a thread to pull.** The group's follow-up is the actual payload.
4. **Turn outward as it escalates.** Late rounds should be aimed at people in the room.
5. **Concepts over slang.** Name the feeling, not the TikTok word for it.
6. **Never assume experience.** No prompt should be unanswerable by someone who hasn't dated.
7. **Sincerity is allowed.** Not everything has to be a joke.
8. **Speakable out loud.** If it needs re-reading, it's cut.

## 4. The 18+ deck — and one line I'm drawing

You asked for genuinely adult, and I agree the current adult deck is far too tame — it reads
like the general deck with the lights dimmed. The rewrite makes it properly explicit.

**18+ truths: fully explicit.** Sex, bodies, preferences, kinks, fantasies, hookups, counts,
first times, worst times, what they want and have never asked for. Frank, adult, unsanitised.
Five spice tiers mapped to the existing intensity ladder so the night still builds:

| Tier | Character |
| --- | --- |
| 1 — Flirty | Attraction, charm, tension |
| 2 — Suggestive | Innuendo, wanting, near-misses |
| 3 — Explicit | Sex openly discussed |
| 4 — Filthy | Specific acts, preferences, kinks |
| 5 — Unhinged | The stuff they'd deny tomorrow |

**18+ dares: intensely charged, but they stop short of instructing sexual contact, nudity, or
photos.** This is the one place I'm not going to do exactly what you asked, so here's my
reasoning rather than a silent downgrade:

A truth is about *that player's own* past and body — they answer or they say nope, and either
way it lands on nobody else. A dare is an instruction performed on someone else in the room,
and the wheel picks that person at random. It cannot know whether they're up for it. A dare
that says "do X to the person on your left" manufactures exactly the pressure the game is
supposed to make it safe to refuse — and it's aimed at the one person who never chose to be
involved.

So adult dares get filthy *language*, not filthy *acts*: describe it in detail, say the
filthiest thing you've said to someone, whisper what you actually noticed first, read out the
message you deleted, confess the scenario that lives rent-free. Charged, revealing, genuinely
adult — without the app instructing anyone to touch anyone.

If you want it to go further than that, tell me and we'll talk about specifically where — but
I'd want the change to be deliberate rather than something I slid in.

## 5. Samples for approval

Judge the tone here. If it's right, I'll write the full bank to this standard.

### CHILL — warm-up, low cost

- What's the dumbest hill you'll die on?
- What's something you're weirdly competitive about that doesn't matter at all?
- What's a compliment you got once and still think about?
- What's the most unhinged thing in your camera roll you'd actually show us?
- What did you believe way too long into adulthood?

### TEA — the ones with a thread to pull

- What's the longest you've been in something you couldn't put a name to?
- What's a rumour about you that was completely true?
- Who here has changed the most since you met them, and be specific?
- What's the last thing you lied about to someone in this room?
- What's something you've pretended not to notice for the sake of peace?
- What's a plan you cancelled with a fake excuse — and the real reason?

### CHAOS — the crash-out zone

- When did you last completely lose it, and what was the actual trigger?
- What's the worst decision you've made after midnight?
- What's something you did that you're amazed you never got caught for?
- What's the biggest scene you've ever caused in public?
- What's a text you've sent that you'd pay money to unsend?

### CRUSH — written so it works if you've never dated

- What's the most delusional reason you've convinced yourself someone liked you?
- What's the smallest thing that's ever given you the ick? Be honest about how petty it was.
- What's a red flag you'd absolutely ignore if you liked someone enough?
- What's a text you typed, stared at, and deleted?
- Who here would you actually take advice from about your love life?

### DEEP — Set III territory, aimed at the room

- What's a question you wish someone would ask you?
- What's something you've never said out loud because you assumed it was too much?
- What do you think people get wrong about you most often?
- Tell the person on your right something you like about them that you've never said.
- What's something you needed to hear at your lowest that nobody said?

### BOLD — outward-facing, high stakes

- Who here would you call first with good news, and who wouldn't make the list?
- What's the most honest thing you could say about this group right now?
- Who's the most confident person here — and is it real or performance?
- What's the one thing you'd change about this friendship group?

### DARES — performance, not stunts

- Give someone here a completely serious compliment, like you're accepting an award for them.
- Do an impression of someone here until they work out it's them.
- Let the group choose one word you must use in every sentence until your next turn.
- Tell the group the moment you decided you actually liked someone here.
- Read the last message you sent out loud, in full, typos included.
- Give a 15-second TED Talk on why you're the most trustworthy person in this room.

### 18+ TRUTHS — tier 2, suggestive

- What's the most recent thing that turned you on that you'd be embarrassed to admit?
- Where's the most inappropriate place you've been genuinely turned on?
- What's the closest you've come to acting on something you shouldn't have?

### 18+ TRUTHS — tier 3, explicit

- What do you actually want in bed that you've never asked for out loud?
- What's the riskiest place you've had sex?
- Do you prefer giving or receiving? No diplomatic answers.
- What's the biggest gap between what you're into and what people assume you're into?
- Have you ever faked it — and did they clock it?

### 18+ TRUTHS — tier 4, filthy

- What's a kink you'd never put in a dating profile?
- Who's the best you've ever had, and what specifically made them better?
- What's the filthiest thing you've ever said out loud, word for word?
- What's something you've done that you've never told a single person about?
- What's the worst sex you've had, and what exactly made it that bad?

### 18+ TRUTHS — tier 5, unhinged

- What's the scenario that lives rent-free in your head?
- Who have you thought about in a way they'd be genuinely shocked by?
- What's the most reckless thing you've done purely because you wanted someone?
- What would you do tonight if there were zero consequences and nobody ever found out?

### 18+ DARES — charged, no contact

- Describe in detail what you'd do to someone you were really into. Don't name them.
- Say the filthiest thing you've ever said to someone, word for word, to the room.
- Whisper to the person on your left the first thing you noticed about them.
- Read out the last flirty message you sent — including the part you deleted.
- Describe your type physically, in detail, with no polite language.
- Tell the group the exact thing that ends the night in your favour.
